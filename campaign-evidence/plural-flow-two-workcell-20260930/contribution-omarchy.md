In a multi-recipient conversation, reply correlation must rest on durable delivery identity: a stable identifier that binds each prompt to the specific session that received it — a delivery or session ID, a receipt, a source revision — carried with the message and echoed in the reply. Attribution should follow that binding end to end, not the position of a message in a shared transcript. Ordering and timestamps are observations about the transcript, not proof of who produced a block.

If an owner infers an answer from recency — grabbing the newest assistant block instead of the one bound to a known delivery identity — misattribution follows immediately. Interleaved or near-simultaneous replies get credited to the wrong session; a slow but correctly delivered answer is silently dropped; a session's contribution can be claimed by another agent that merely happened to write last. Correlation becomes a guess about timing rather than a record of delivery, and any downstream decision built on "who answered" inherits that error.

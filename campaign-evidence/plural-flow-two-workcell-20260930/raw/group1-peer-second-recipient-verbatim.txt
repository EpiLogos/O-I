Reply correlation must rest on durable delivery identity: stable, verifiable identifiers — a recipient/session key, a request or message ID, and an explicit binding between the request and the reply — carried in the message itself. Those identifiers are what let an owner say "this reply came from this session, answering that specific request," independent of arrival order or timing.

Recency is not identity. In a concurrent multi-recipient conversation, assistant blocks interleave, arrive out of order, or land near-simultaneously; the newest block may belong to a different session entirely, or several replies may be in flight at once. If an owner infers authorship from "the latest assistant block," several things break: attribution (a reply is credited to the wrong agent), provenance and audit trails (records no longer reflect who actually answered), routing (follow-ups go to the wrong recipient), and deduplication or idempotency (one reply counted twice, another ignored). Correlation errors then compound silently — a reply can be delivered yet never attributed to the session that produced it, which is exactly what this test must not allow.

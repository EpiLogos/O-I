# Source study: Hermann Hesse, *The Glass Bead Game* (Das Glasperlenspiel, 1943)

Study of the Game as Hesse actually gives it — mechanics, evolution and decay,
ladder-character — and of its afterlife in computing, kept distinct from
interpretation, as a source for the Digital Logos Workstation (DLW) conception
of the epilogos system.

## Why this study exists

The owner had long carried a relation between the epilogos system — the real
name of the O:I work, whose current shell form is a full DLW, Digital Logos
Workstation — and Hesse's Glass Bead Game. No record of that relation existed
in the ground. This study is its first durable anchor, commissioned
2026-10-10: "a really deep research not just into the idea of it but the
actual game mechanics, nature, essence, evolution (which holds the meaning of
its decay), and the character of it as a 'ladder to be left behind'."

**The figure's referent (owner clarification, 2026-10-11):** Epi-Logos is a
philosophical project — the corpus (the essay, QL/MEF, Bimba, M/M′, Vāk,
Ta-Onta, Nara, Epii, the Covenant) as the **esoteric identity**; the system
is the **exoteric identity**; the mode is the projection of the one over
the other. The corpus is the owner's game and the native game of the
system. See [06-THE-PRAXIS.md](06-THE-PRAXIS.md), §0.

## Method

Four parallel research lanes, each verifying against primary text:

1. **Mechanics** — what the novel specifies about the Game's apparatus and
   practice, scene by scene.
2. **Evolution and decay** — the Game's internal history as the Introduction
   tells it, the decay theses voiced inside the novel, and the external
   composition history (1931–1943).
3. **Ladder-character** — Knecht's arc, "Stufen", and the ladder/raft/ferry
   lineage, with Hesse–Wittgenstein link status checked.
4. **Computational afterlife** — hypertext, semantic-web and AI-era
   invocations; the catalog of named attempts and their outcomes.

All novel quotations were verified against the full text of the Richard and
Clara Winston translation (Holt, 1969; Bantam edition with Ziolkowski's
foreword), accessed at
[archive.org/details/hesseludi](https://archive.org/details/hesseludi), cited
by chapter. Scholarly claims are labelled. Anything that could not be
verified is marked UNVERIFIED rather than dropped silently. Several
popular-image claims were checked and **falsified**; they are recorded as
corrections (see [01-MECHANICS.md](01-MECHANICS.md), §4).

## Standing caveats

- The novel's own Introduction declares that no textbook of the Game "will
  ever be written"; everything here about "mechanics" is reconstruction from
  a literary text that withholds its rules by design.
- Hesse resisted normativising readings (letters to Staiger, Faesi,
  Pannwitz); the Game is finally satire — Knecht walks out of Castalia.
- The 60-year record of implementation attempts is a consistent negative
  result, documented in lane 04; the study treats it as evidence, not as
  discouragement.

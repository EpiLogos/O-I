#Device UI

You play a device while your attention is on the material. The agent runs, the field moves, the torus rings, and your eyes are there, not on the device. So a device must show its state at a glance, answer the hand at once, and never ask you to read. Live solved this. Every Live device is one panel of one height: a picture of what the device does, with handles on it, and the controls set round the picture. You learn one panel and you can read all of them.

The DLW draws every device the same way: one head, one height, a picture with handles, and the same few controls round it. What a device does is in its catalogue entry. How it looks and how it answers your hand is set out here, and five reference devices run live in §9.

#1. What a device panel is

A designed face, not a form. A device's parameters are its contract with its owner. Its panel is drawn by hand for that device, as every Live device is. A list of parameters drawn one under the other is the fallback, and only for a device that ships no panel of its own. Live does the same for a plug-in that has no editor.

The picture comes first. Most panels have a display: a dark well that draws the device's state from its live values. EQ Eight draws its curve, Auto Filter its filter, Wavetable its wave. Handles in the well are controls: drag the picture and the parameter moves. The other controls stand round the display, in sections.

One height, many widths. On a chain, every device has the same height. The width comes from what the device holds. A small effect is narrow, and an instrument with two displays is wide.

Nothing scrolls, and no box sits inside a box. Sections are columns, ruled off from each other by a hairline and named in small capitals. The well is the only inset surface. When a panel holds more than fits, it gets pages in its head, as Wavetable has Osc 1 and Osc 2.

Values are short. A control shows a number, a unit or a word. A sentence never appears in a panel. What a control does is said in the status bar when you point at it.

#2. One device, five views

A device has one set of values and five views. All five show the same values. A change in one view shows in the other four at once.

View Where Size What it holds Live's nearest
Folded A chain 22 px wide, chain height The activator, the name, up to two short readings, read downward. A folded device.
Compact A chain Chain height. The width comes from its sections. The display and up to four macros. A rack with only its macros showing; a small device such as Utility.
Whole The pool under the browser The pool's width, one or two bands high. Pages when more is needed. Every parameter, grouped, and the display with all its handles. The full panel of a device, with its pages.
Full A pane The pane The material itself: the page, the field, the torus, the world. No controls. Wavetable's expanded view; a plug-in window.
Widget The rail A tile of 136 × 136 px, or 280 × 136 px A small display and up to three readings or macros. Live has none. Push's display is the nearest.
  • Press a plate on a chain to show its device whole in the pool.
  • Press ⤢ to open the device full in a pane.
  • Press ▾ in the head to fold the device. Press the folded title to open it.
  • A pane holds no device controls. They stay in Device view under the pane. When you maximise the pane to the window, the device can show its own controls inside it.
  • A widget stays on the rail when you change mode. You choose what its tile shows.

#4. Anatomy

#4.1 The head

From left to right:

  1. Activator. A round light. Amber: the owner reports the device on. Grey: the owner reports it off. A hollow ring: the owner has not said. Press it to ask the owner to turn the device on or off. The light changes only when the owner answers.
  2. Fold ▾.
  3. Name. A rack shows its set's name: "Skills · build-lane".
  4. Held mark. The name of whoever holds values on this device, in the stall colour.
  5. Pages. One tab for each page. The shown page joins the body's face. A page can carry its own light, as Hybrid Reverb's EQ tab does.
  6. Open full ⤢, at the right.

Right-click the head for Save as preset, Hot-swap, Show automation and Map.

#4.2 The body

The body is one band on a chain and one or two bands in the pool. A band is a row of sections. A section is a column of controls with a label. The display stands in a section of its own, at the left.

#4.3 Colours

Colour Means
Face The body of the plate.
Head Lighter than the face. Tinted blue when the device is selected.
Field The ground of value boxes, toggles and choosers.
Well The display. Near black.
Teal A value: a dial's arc, a value box's fill, a curve, a reach for reading.
Amber On: a toggle, a chosen radio button, the activator, a handle, a reach for writing.
Green An owner's yes: a skill admitted, a task done.
Orange Someone else holds this value.
Red Automation, and the record button.

A device does not bring colours of its own. A colour inside a display carries a meaning of the material, as a track's colour does.

#5.1 The hand

The same moves work on every control that holds a number.

Do Result
Drag up or down The value moves. The whole range is 160 px. A stepped value moves one step for each 14 px.
Hold Shift while you drag The value moves ten times more slowly.
Double-click The value returns to its default.
Focus it and press ↑ ↓ or ← → One step, or 1 % of the range.
Page Up, Page Down 10 % of the range.
Home, End The minimum, the maximum.
Delete The default.
Point at it The status bar says its name and what it does.

A handle moves in the directions that its picture shows. A handle for two parameters, such as gravity across and up, moves in two directions. The arrow keys move a focused handle 2 px, or 0.5 px with Shift.

#5.2 A change and its answer

While you drag, the control shows the value you set. When you release it, the new value goes to the owner; a key press sends at once. Until the owner confirms the value, it shows in italics. If the owner refuses, the control returns to the owner's value, and the status bar gives the owner's reason.

Some changes ask you first. When you raise a scope's reach, the new reach waits, drawn dashed, with Confirm and Cancel beside it. Nothing is sent until you confirm.

#6. Standing

Besides its value, a control shows who moves it.

Standing How it shows What you do
Automated A red dot at the control's top left. Move it to override the lane.
Overridden A hollow red dot. The transport's Re-enable lights. Press Re-enable to give the value back to its lane.
Modulated A thin outer arc on the dial: the range that the modulator moves the value through. The status bar names the modulator. Set the centre of the range with the dial.
Held The arc or the fill in orange, and the holder's name under the control. Press the name to give the value back.
Waiting The value in italics, the arc dashed. Wait for the owner, or move it again.
Disabled The control at 40 % strength. The status bar says why. Do what the reason says.
No reading A hollow light. Nothing. The owner has not said.

#7. The display

  • Draw every mark from the device's values or readings, by the device's own law. Do not draw a likely picture.
  • When a reading is missing, say which one in the well. Do not draw it as zero.
  • Give a compact display at least one handle: the one parameter that you most often move. Give a whole display a handle for each parameter that the picture can show.
  • Number the handles 1, 2 and on, in order of importance.
  • Put axis labels inside the well, small and faint. Put one short reading at the top right.
  • Move the picture only when its readings move. Respect a request for reduced motion.
  • A rack's picture is its chain list.

#8. What a device supplies

A device brings these to the shell:

  1. Its parameters, each declared under the device's own id: key, name, kind, range or values, default, unit, group, whether it is a macro, and its write path. A parameter without a write path is a reading.
  2. Its five views, built from the panel kit: folded, compact, whole, full and widget.
  3. Its displays and their handles.

The shell brings the values, the writes, the navigation between views, and the standing of each parameter: its automation, its modulator, its holder.

The panel kit is packages/live-shell/ui/src/inhabitants/sdk/panel.tsx. A device without views of its own is drawn by the generic plate: its parameters, grouped, in the order the device declares them.

#8.1 A parameter earns its place

A parameter stays on a device only when all of these are true:

  • An owner operation stands behind it: a write that the owner exposes, or a reading that the owner reports. A word for a quality, such as thoroughness or boldness, is not a parameter unless such an operation stands behind it.
  • Its control fits its values. Use a dial or a value box for a quantity that varies smoothly. Use a stepped dial, a radio or a chooser for a few states, and a toggle for two. Do not draw a range or a step that the owner does not have.
  • When you move it, something that you can see or hear changes: the display, the material or a reading.
  • Its default is the owner's default.
  • A macro moves real parameters, each within its own range.

A device whose owner has no smooth quantity has no dials. Its compact controls are its real discrete ones. A rack of skills has no risk knob: it has your requests, a scope, and a preview that you apply.

#8.2 Kind marks

A device that holds things of several kinds shows each thing's kind, not a picture of its name. Carried practice has four kinds, each with its woodcut mark: a skill, a method, a methodology and a skillset (manual §19.6). The person chooses one of the two sets of marks, the blade or the pen, and the whole shell uses it. A mark has three states, and its drawing does not change: a full line for available, the line in orange for held back for a reason that you can clear, a dim line for withheld.

#10. When a device's UI is done

A device's UI is done when all of these are true:

  • All five views draw in the device gallery on the device's declared parameters.
  • The five views move together.
  • Every control says what it does in the status bar.
  • Nothing scrolls inside a plate, and no box sits inside a box.
  • The compact view has four macros or fewer.
  • The display draws from values, and its handles move the parameters they show.
  • Every control works from the keyboard.
  • The views match the device's catalogue entry.
  • Beside its Live nearest, the plate reads as a Live device.

To see a device in the gallery, run the gallery from packages/live-shell/ui:

npx vite --config src/inhabitants/catalogue/vite.config.mjs

Then open ?device=<family>:<face>&view=all. Add &generic=1 to see the generic plate of the same device.

#11. The runtime

  • The device runtime draws a device's own views when it has them, and the generic plate when it does not.
  • The runtime has one size for each of the five views. Whole holds the controls. Full holds the material.
  • The panel kit draws the head, the sections and the controls of every plate, generic plates included. An instrument's sections are bands and sections.
  • A change goes to the owner when you release the control, as §5.2 says. No plate has a Send button.
  • A field device of Expressions uses its field diagram as its display.
